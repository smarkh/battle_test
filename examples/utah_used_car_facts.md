# Case information — fictional test case (Utah)

All names, businesses and events below are invented for testing.

- **Client (plaintiff):** Daniel Okafor, 655 E 2nd St, Ogden, Utah (Weber County). He bought the car for personal and family use.
- **Other party (defendant):** Wasatch Auto Outlet LLC, a Utah limited liability company and licensed used motor vehicle dealer in Ogden, Utah (Weber County).
- **Sale:** On May 3, 2026, Okafor bought a used 2022 Honda Pilot from Wasatch for $21,900, paid in full with a credit union loan (loan papers available). The window Buyers Guide had the "As Is — No Dealer Warranty" box checked, and the written purchase agreement repeats "sold as is."
- **What the dealer said:** The online listing said "clean title, accident-free, no flood damage." On May 1, 2026, the salesperson, Brandon Sorensen, texted Okafor: "Clean Carfax, never been in a flood, I checked it myself." Okafor has the screenshots. He was told nothing about any insurance claim.
- **Problems:** From late June 2026 the dashboard warning lights flickered and the power windows failed. On July 14, 2026, an independent mechanic found silt under the carpets, corrosion on wiring connectors and a water line inside the door panels, and wrote that the car had been submerged.
- **History:** A vehicle history report Okafor bought on July 15, 2026 shows a Texas insurer declared the car a total loss after flood damage in October 2025. It was retitled in another state without a flood brand before Wasatch bought it at auction on April 2, 2026. The auction's condition report given to Wasatch was marked "flood/total loss — announced."
- **Value and repair:** The mechanic's written estimate to replace the corroded wiring and modules is $9,800. A written appraisal values the car, with its flood history, at $11,000.
- **Demand:** On July 21, 2026, Okafor gave Wasatch written notice of the flood history and asked it to take the car back and refund the price. Wasatch's manager refused in writing on July 24, 2026, pointing to the "as is" terms. Okafor still has the car and has stopped driving it.
- **What the client wants:** to return the car and get his money back, or the loss in value and repair costs, plus loan interest and fees he has paid, attorney fees, and any other recovery available.
