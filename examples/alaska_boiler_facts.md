# Case information — fictional test case (Alaska)

All names, businesses and events below are invented for testing.

- **Client (plaintiff):** Ruth Nakamura, homeowner, 3127 Birch Hollow Rd, Fairbanks, Alaska (Fourth Judicial District).
- **Other party (defendant):** Northern Lights Heating & Plumbing LLC, an Alaska limited liability company based in North Pole, Alaska.
- **Contract:** On August 11, 2026, Nakamura and Northern Lights signed a written contract to remove her old oil boiler and install a new boiler and baseboard heating loop for $16,800. Completion was due by September 15, 2026. The contract says the work will be "performed in a good and workmanlike manner and to code" and that Northern Lights will "keep the home heated or provide temporary heat during the changeover."
- **Payment:** Nakamura paid an $8,400 deposit on August 13, 2026 (bank record available).
- **Work:** Northern Lights removed the old boiler on September 8, 2026, left a portable electric heater, and did not return. Nakamura called and texted the owner, Gary Holm, on September 10, 14 and 19. On September 19 Holm texted back: "Parts are stuck in Anchorage, we'll be out when we can."
- **Damage:** On September 27, 2026 the temperature dropped to 4°F overnight. The heater could not keep up, and pipes froze and burst in the kitchen and a bathroom. A water-damage contractor's written estimate for the repairs is $14,200.
- **Replacement contractor:** Nakamura hired Tanana Valley Mechanical, which installed a new boiler and heating loop on October 6, 2026 for $19,300 (invoice available).
- **Demand:** On October 9, 2026, Nakamura sent Northern Lights a written demand for the $8,400 deposit and the water-damage costs. Northern Lights has not responded.
- **What the client wants:** the deposit back, the extra cost of the replacement contractor, the water-damage repair costs, and any other damages available.
