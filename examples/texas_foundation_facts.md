# Case information — fictional test case (Texas)

All names, businesses and events below are invented for testing.

- **Client (plaintiff):** Marcus Reyes, homeowner, 4107 Pecan Hollow Drive, Austin, Texas (Travis County).
- **Other party (defendant):** Lone Star Foundation Solutions LLC, a Texas limited liability company based in Round Rock, Texas.
- **Contract:** On February 2, 2026, Reyes and Lone Star signed a written contract for foundation repair on his house for $19,000. It calls for 14 concrete pressed piers "driven to refusal, minimum depth 12 feet," and includes a "lifetime transferable warranty against settlement at the repaired areas."
- **Sales pitch:** Before he signed, Lone Star's salesperson, Kyle Brandt, told Reyes in person and by text on January 28, 2026: "This is guaranteed for life. Once we lift it, your foundation will never move again."
- **Payment:** Reyes paid $9,500 on signing (bank record available). The remaining $9,500 was due on completion and has not been paid.
- **Work:** Lone Star's crew installed the piers on February 16–18, 2026 and said the job was complete.
- **Problems:** By April 2026, the existing cracks in the interior walls were wider, a new crack appeared in the brick exterior, and two doors would no longer close. On April 21, 2026, Lone Star inspected, blamed "abnormal soil movement," declined to do any warranty work, and demanded the remaining $9,500.
- **Engineer's report:** On May 12, 2026, Reyes hired a licensed structural engineer, whose written report says 9 of the 14 piers stopped at 6 to 8 feet, short of the contract's 12-foot minimum, and that this is the likely cause of the continued settlement.
- **Repair estimate:** Another foundation company quoted $27,400 to remove and replace the piers and repair the cosmetic damage (written estimate available).
- **Demand:** On June 10, 2026, Reyes sent Lone Star a detailed written demand letter by certified mail, describing the defects and the damages claimed. Lone Star has not responded.
- **What the client wants:** the cost of fixing the foundation properly, the $9,500 he paid back or credited, attorney fees, and any other recovery available.
