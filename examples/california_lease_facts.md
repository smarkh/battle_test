# Case information — fictional test case (California)

All names, businesses and events below are invented for testing.

- **Client (plaintiff):** Aisha Thompson, former tenant, now living at 2210 Fulton St, Berkeley, California.
- **Other party (defendant):** Bayview Residential Properties LLC, a California limited liability company that owns and manages the apartment building at 418 Lakeshore Ave, Oakland, California (Alameda County).
- **Lease:** On January 15, 2024, Thompson and Bayview signed a written two-year lease of Apartment 3B at 418 Lakeshore Ave, as her home, from February 1, 2024 to January 31, 2026, at $2,700 a month. The lease says the prevailing party in any action on the lease recovers reasonable attorney fees.
- **Deposit:** Thompson paid a $5,400 security deposit on January 15, 2024 (bank record available). A move-in checklist signed by both sides notes "carpet worn in living room."
- **Move-out:** Thompson moved out on January 31, 2026, after professional cleaning (receipt available), and returned the keys that day. On the same day she gave Bayview her forwarding address in writing. She took dated photos and video of every room. Bayview did not take photographs of the unit at move-out.
- **What Bayview did:** Bayview sent nothing within 21 days. On March 30, 2026, it emailed that it was keeping the entire $5,400 for "repainting and carpet replacement," with no itemized statement, receipts or invoices. Thompson had lived there two years, and the walls had only nail holes from pictures.
- **Demand:** On April 15, 2026, Thompson sent Bayview a written demand for the full deposit. Bayview's property manager, Greg Lassiter, replied by email on April 20, 2026: "We keep deposits to cover turnover. That's our policy."
- **What the client wants:** the $5,400 deposit, statutory damages for bad-faith retention, interest, attorney fees, and any other recovery available.
